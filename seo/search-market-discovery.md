# Fathers Front Search-Market Discovery Program

## Purpose

Discover the urgent questions fathers ask, verify which questions are also
searched, determine which ones Steve can answer credibly, and turn the strongest
problem clusters into content and product opportunities.

This is not a one-time automated keyword export. It is a guided research cycle
run one category at a time.

## Division of responsibility

### Steve leads

- Selects and defines the category
- Explains the lived reality behind the problem
- Identifies dangerous, misleading, or legally sensitive framing
- Determines whether Fathers Front has a useful and credible answer
- Supplies the actual answer, experience, examples, and point of view
- Approves content and product conclusions

### Codex leads

- Expands seed topics into research queries
- Collects public questions and search language in bulk
- Deduplicates and normalizes the question bank
- Groups questions by problem, intent, urgency, and journey stage
- Validates search demand with available keyword data
- Reviews current results and identifies weak or missing answers
- Produces a prioritized opportunity report for Steve to review

Codex does not invent Steve's expert answer. It can organize, test, challenge,
and draft from the answer Steve provides.

## Research cycle

Run the following seven steps for one category before beginning another.

### Step 1: Define the category with Steve

Complete `category-brief-template.md`. Establish:

- Who has this problem
- What has happened
- What decision or result they need
- What language Steve already hears
- What Fathers Front can and cannot responsibly answer
- Which offer could eventually help, without forcing a product assumption

### Step 2: Build the seed set

Create 20 to 40 seed phrases using:

- The problem
- The desired result
- The feared result
- The task being attempted
- The person or institution involved
- Relevant legal and nonlegal terminology
- Plain-language and emotionally charged variants

Do not require `father` or `dad` in every seed. A father searching for help may
use a general query such as `how to prepare for a custody hearing`.

### Step 3: Collect questions from five evidence sources

#### Google search behavior

Collect autocomplete suggestions, People Also Ask questions, related searches,
featured-snippet questions, and recurring titles in the results.

#### YouTube search behavior

Collect autocomplete suggestions, successful video titles, recurring questions
in comments, and topics with strong engagement but weak answers.

#### Question communities

Review Quora, Reddit, public legal-question sites, forums, and other indexed
discussions. Record the question in the user's words and preserve its source.

#### Social communities

With authorized access, review relevant Facebook groups and other private
communities. Record only anonymized questions and patterns. Never copy names,
personal case details, children's information, or private posts into the repo.

#### Existing Fathers Front evidence

Review anonymized intake questions, emails, comments, advisory calls,
Brotherhood discussions, and Search Console queries when available.

Questions from communities demonstrate pain and language. They do not prove
Google search volume. Search behavior and keyword data must validate demand.

### Step 4: Normalize the question bank

Store findings in `question-bank-template.csv`. Keep the original wording, then
add a normalized version so variants can be clustered without losing the voice
of the market.

Tag every question by:

- Category and subcategory
- Source
- Search intent
- Journey stage
- Urgency
- Father-specific relevance
- Legal sensitivity
- Answer status

### Step 5: Validate search demand

Use DataForSEO and available Google data to measure:

- Search volume and trend
- Related queries
- Cost per click
- Competition
- Search-result composition
- Pages and domains currently ranking

Zero or unavailable volume does not automatically invalidate a question. It may
still be valuable when it repeats across communities, has high urgency, or is
part of a larger searchable cluster.

### Step 6: Conduct the answer interview

Review each promising cluster with Steve and assign one answer status:

- `Proven`: Steve has direct experience and a clear answer
- `Collaborative`: Steve has a strong framework but needs professional input
- `Researchable`: a responsible answer can be built from authoritative sources
- `Experience only`: useful personal perspective, not universal instruction
- `Do not answer`: outside scope, unsafe, or likely to create legal confusion

For every approved cluster, capture:

1. The direct answer
2. What most people misunderstand
3. What depends on facts, jurisdiction, or professional advice
4. The first practical action
5. Common mistakes
6. Steve's lived example or point of view
7. The appropriate next step

### Step 7: Prioritize opportunities

Score each cluster from 1 to 5 on:

- Demonstrated demand
- Urgency
- Fathers Front relevance
- Steve's authority
- Weakness of existing answers
- Content potential
- Lead-magnet potential
- Paid-product potential

Do not automatically select the highest-volume topic. Favor the overlap of real
demand, urgent need, credible authority, and a useful next step.

## Deliverable for each category

Codex produces a category opportunity report containing:

- Market-language summary
- Complete deduplicated question bank
- Ten highest-priority question clusters
- Search-demand evidence and limitations
- Existing-result weaknesses
- Questions Steve can answer now
- Questions requiring an attorney or other professional collaborator
- Recommended cornerstone article and supporting content
- Video opportunities
- Lead-magnet hypothesis
- Information-product hypothesis
- Natural connection to an existing offer, if one exists
- Questions rejected and why

## Initial category sequence

1. Custody and parenting time
2. Finding and managing attorneys
3. Pro se and case preparation
4. Evidence, documentation, and allegations
5. Child support and financial pressure
6. Alienation and the father-child relationship
7. High-conflict co-parenting and communication
8. Abduction, relocation, and jurisdiction
9. Divorce, identity, and reconstruction

Begin with one category. Do not collect thousands of unreviewed questions across
all categories at once.
